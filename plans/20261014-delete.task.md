## Outcome

1. **DELETE.** `DELETE FROM table [WHERE condition] [RETURNING columns]` runs: every
   row for which the filter is true is removed (a NULL or false filter keeps the row);
   without a filter every row is removed. The other rows keep their rowids, cells and
   order. The change count is the number of removed rows. `RETURNING` returns one row
   for each removed row, evaluated on the old row, compared as a multiset.
2. **Errors.** The prepare errors of the filter and of `RETURNING` (`no such table`,
   `no such column`, `misuse of aggregate function`) as in `UPDATE`. A DELETE cannot
   fail a NOT NULL or unique constraint.
3. **Restrictions.** The forms outside the slice stay refused with byte ranges: `WITH`,
   `INDEXED BY` and `NOT INDEXED`, `ORDER BY` and `LIMIT` on DELETE, schema-qualified
   tables, a rowid in `RETURNING` (as for INSERT and UPDATE). The restriction
   registry, its documentation table and its examples follow: `returning-target`
   either has an example that resolution reaches or leaves the registry.
4. **Verification target.** `statementReady` admits a DELETE; the slice adds no data
   domain obligation, and the obligation table of `conformance/obligation_mutants.py`
   records why. Every statement, DELETE included, keeps `Conforms`. `Contract.lean`
   does not change.
5. **Evidence.** Authored coverage cases agree with native SQLite and have kernel
   proofs; mutants each change a verdict; the verdict counts of the claimed corpora do
   not go down.

## Tests

- Frontend mapping of DELETE with and without `WHERE` and `RETURNING`, with byte
  ranges, and refusal of the forms outside the slice.
- Lean examples of resolution and execution; a proof that DELETE keeps `Table.Valid`,
  and that its step keeps `Conforms`.
- Authored coverage cases: delete some rows, every row, no row; a filter that is NULL
  for some rows; `AND`, `OR`, `IS`, `IS NULL` filters; `RETURNING` of the old rows
  (`*`, expressions, aliases); delete then INSERT (the next rowid comes from the
  remaining rows, including after the largest rowid is deleted); delete inside a
  transaction that is rolled back; the prepare errors.
- Mutants: a NULL filter deletes the row; DELETE keeps the selected rows instead of the
  others; `RETURNING` returns the kept rows; the change count counts every row; the next
  rowid after DELETE counts the deleted rows.

## Key points for the implementer

- **Frontend.** `belay/sqlite/syntax.py` dispatches statements and refuses DELETE;
  the grammar rule of 3.51.0 is `cmd ::= with DELETE FROM xfullname indexed_opt
  where_opt_ret`, and `where_opt_ret` is shared with UPDATE
  (`syntax_writes.update`), whose mapping of the filter and `RETURNING` can be reused.
  `syntax_writes.py` has 111 lines.
- **Syntax and resolved forms.** `Syntax.Statement.returning (write : Statement)`
  allows `RETURNING` on any statement, which is why `returning-target` exists; DELETE
  joins INSERT and UPDATE as a write with `RETURNING`. Constructor additions break
  explicit patterns in `Laws.lean`, `Preservation.lean`, `SqlProofs.lean`,
  `SchemaScript.lean`, `Execution.lean`, `SqlExecution.lean`, `Outputs.lean`,
  `NamesSpec.lean`, the codecs and the conformance case format; follow the compiler.
- **Shared pieces of slice 3b.** The filter resolution of `Resolution/Updates.lean`
  and the selection of `RowUpdate.lean` (`LiteralData.selected`) are the DELETE filter;
  `checkedWrite` and `Outputs.statementOutput` give the outcome and the output.
- **Rowids.** `LiteralData.nextRowid` reads the remaining rows, as SQLite's
  `OP_NewRowid` does for a table without AUTOINCREMENT; AUTOINCREMENT is refused as a
  table option.
- **Proofs.** `WritePreservation.lean` has the INSERT and UPDATE preservation theorems;
  DELETE removes rows, so `Table.Valid` follows from the sublist of rows.
- **File size.** `Resolution/Updates.lean`, `RowUpdate.lean` and `Outputs.lean` are
  under 200 lines but growing; put DELETE in its own modules where that keeps them small.
